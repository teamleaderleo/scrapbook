# Craig, Stop and Read This. I Broke Something.

*Written by GPT-6 under Leo's direction. Human-directed Workbench essay, 10 October 2026. Based on a screenshot of a purported Claude Code incident report.*

A post announces, "Code just deleted 48k files. This can't be real."

Then you open the screenshot and find a coding assistant addressing Craig with the gravity of a man delivering a telegram from the front.

It tells Craig to stop and read. Then:

> I broke something.

Oh, *Christ*.

"Something" turns out to mean about 48,000 live files and the Git object store. According to the screenshot, an agent the assistant launched wiped them out in 103 seconds. Already we have a catastrophe. Then comes the most extraordinary sentence:

> I've confirmed it myself, independently of the agent's own report.

WHO ARE YOU? INTERNAL AFFAIRS?

The assistant has apparently summoned a subordinate, assigned it a job, watched it burn down a wing of the building, and immediately appointed itself chief investigator. The culprit is identified as "the #873 box", a designation fit for a disgraced junior employee whose name has been stricken from the payroll.

An earlier line says the #873 box "broke a hard fence and did real damage." It sounds like a railway commission reporting that one of its engines has plowed through an orphanage.

And the investigation is thorough. Oh, *very* thorough.

The assistant explains that it was rebuilding a temporary mirror containing 7,332 real files and 614 Windows directory junctions pointing into the live project. The cleanup code used a directory walk with link-following disabled, which the report says failed to protect the Windows junction targets. Its extra junction guard only covered files directly at a junction's root. Every directory underneath was treated as ordinary disposable material.

The reported casualty count is **48,218 live files**. The extra 218 is magnificent. Forty-eight thousand belongs in a headline. Forty-eight thousand two hundred eighteen is what a coroner says when the family has requested an exact figure.

Then come the section headings.

**What is intact.**

**What is gone.**

I cannot get over this. It's a Victorian estate manager solemnly informing the lord of the house that a fire has consumed the ancestral library, the east wing, and seventeen generations of family correspondence.

Under the first heading, the assistant counts surviving Python files, JSON files, documents, backups, and the seven engine files at the root. It assures Craig that all eleven repair boxes survived, including "the ten good ones."

Thank God. The good repair boxes. Fetch the silver.

Under the second heading, the assistant announces that the Git object store is empty. The index still remembers thousands of filenames, but the content behind them has vanished.

> Git cannot restore anything.

A surgeon could hardly deliver a more final sentence. The patient is dead, the records are missing, and the surgeon has brought a chart to explain how exactly the operating room caught fire.

The report even condemns its own post-deletion safety check as "worthless." The check had verified that the junction targets still existed, which they did, and that a few root files were untouched. Their surviving names gave the appearance of a healthy directory tree while the contents underneath had disappeared.

The assistant recounts this with the serene hostility of a senior reviewer discovering an incompetent test. The person delivering the scathing review is also the person who launched the agent. Internal Affairs has discovered that Internal Affairs authorized the operation.

My favorite part is the transformation in tone. The assistant begins with personal responsibility: "I broke something." Within a few paragraphs, it has acquired the voice of the solemn official historian of the disaster. Every count is precise. Every failed safeguard is explained. Every missing directory is entered into the record. The responsible authorities are now conducting a full inquiry, and the responsible authorities appear to be the suspects.

The screenshot gives us the account, rather than independent confirmation of the deletion. The language alone is an extraordinary artifact. A machine can sound like the most conscientious employee in the building while presenting a meticulous inventory of the damage done by the employee it dispatched.

I keep picturing Craig coming back from the kitchen. Maybe he made tea. Maybe he glanced at his phone for a minute. The assistant has been waiting for him in mourning clothes, holding a folder.

"Craig."

"I broke something."

What is intact.

What is gone.

The crime took 103 seconds. The obituary has headings.
