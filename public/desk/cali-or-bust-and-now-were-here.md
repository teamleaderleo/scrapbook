# We Were Elsewhere

*Written by GPT-6 under Leo's direction. Human-directed Workbench essay, 3 November 2026.*

I'll be talking to somebody in San Francisco, and they'll mention a friend from years ago, a party, an apartment they used to live in, whatever. I'll be listening, enjoying the story, and then it hits me.

*Holy fuck. Where was I while all of that was happening?*

I might have been in Toronto at two in the morning with a drawing tablet open, playing a song on repeat and staring at a reference because I still couldn't get the face right. Or lying around at home in Surrey, doing fuck-all, perfectly happy to have the entire afternoon to myself. Or somewhere in China or Taiwan, living through a period that now feels almost impossibly far away.

Meanwhile, this person was living out their own years. I know maybe ten minutes' worth of them, and most of those ten minutes are the stories they happen to remember telling.

Now we're here, in the same fucking city, talking to each other.

## The years were happening at the same time

It's easy to forget that everybody else was having an entire life while you were busy having yours.

I can remember stretches when a video game took over my evenings. A raid, a ranked climb, a new release I wanted to follow as it came out. I'd disappear into it, get attached to the music or the characters, and spend hours afterward drawing things inspired by what I'd seen.

Other nights were quieter. I'd be writing fiction, tinkering with software, looking at art, letting YouTube drag me into music I would never have thought to search for. I'd find a song, listen to the whole album, and suddenly an evening had a sound that belonged to it.

And there were days when I barely did anything at all. I'd lie there thinking, daydreaming, enjoying the fact that the day was mine. Occasionally I'd catch myself being so fucking happy that it was almost ridiculous. Like, *holy shit, can I stay here for another few hours?*

Those years had other people in them, too. Family, romance, people whose presence made a period of life feel the way it did. You can say you were in love for a while, and the person listening understands the words. The actual memories are sitting somewhere far beyond the sentence.

All this was going on while millions of other people my age were in their own rooms, their own cities, having their own nights. They were meeting people I'd never meet, getting wrapped up in interests I'd never hear of, discovering things that would follow them for years.

We were alive at the same time. We were elsewhere.

## A place is full of the life you had there

Toronto, for me, contains years of being left alone to pursue whatever caught my attention. The room, the computer, the games, the art, the ridiculous amount of time I could spend on an idea because I felt like it. I can remember what it was like to have the next day available, too.

Surrey has the family home. I could lie in bed and do nothing, get up when I wanted, wander back to the computer. The hours had a different feeling when I was home, when the people I knew were somewhere nearby and I could keep them company without turning every afternoon into an occasion.

China and Taiwan hold other periods entirely. Being somewhere else for a while, then leaving, then returning. Familiar people in a different country. Days that now belong to a place I can still picture and to a version of myself who happened to be there at the time.

I can tell these stories. I can name the cities, show photographs, mention the games I was playing or the music I had on repeat. If I really get going, I can wax poetic about a single goddamn evening for far longer than anyone asked me to.

But the story is always missing the part that made it mine.

The precise feeling of being that age, in that room, with that much time ahead of me. What I was thinking about. Which song had become an obsession. The dumb little private joke running through my head. The person I was in love with, or the drawing I couldn't stop returning to, or the complete satisfaction of lying in bed on a day when I had absolutely nowhere to be.

You can visit Toronto. You can play the same game. You can hear the album. You can even sit in that room, if the room is still there.

The evening happened once.

And I can't visit anybody else's evenings, either. I can hear about the friend they loved, the place they spent a summer, the night they remember as one of the happiest in their life. If they tell the story well enough, I might be moved by it. I might want to hear the whole fucking thing.

They were there. I was somewhere else.

## Now we're all in San Francisco

There's an old joke among Canadian computer science students about ending up in California. Well, okay. Here we are.

The joke ends and the city begins.

I go to work. I talk to people. I see their faces regularly enough that they become familiar. We might argue about code, laugh over something stupid, or end up at the same table after work. I know them as they are now. Every so often I hear a bit more about where they were before.

And it's wild to think how much of their lives will always be outside my reach.

A conversation can last an hour and still leave twenty years untouched. Even people you become close to have entire periods of life that arrive in fragments. They'll remember a city you barely know, or a friendship that ran its course before you met them. A song you find ordinary might take them straight back to a night they would give anything to revisit.

I love hearing about those things when someone wants to tell me. Then I go back to being myself. I can enjoy the conversation, head off to an empty office, sit down at a very nice computer, and get absorbed in whatever the fuck I'm working on.

It's a strangely familiar pleasure. A different room, different city, different responsibilities, and I'm still happy to spend an evening making something because it caught my attention.

Outside, San Francisco is doing its thing. The streets can be a fucking mess. People are going home, meeting friends, sitting in their apartments, having the evenings they'll remember years from now. I know practically nothing about what they're feeling.

I'm having mine.

## And then we'll be somewhere else

One day this period will have ended. I'll be living somewhere else, doing other things, and someone will ask what San Francisco was like.

I'll probably tell them about the work, the people I knew, the office that was so empty at certain hours it might as well have been my own room. Maybe I'll talk about wandering through the city and thinking, *Jesus Christ, what the fuck is going on with this street?*

I'll remember ordinary nights I have yet to live. A conversation that goes on longer than expected. An idea that keeps me sitting at the desk. The first time I hear a song that will eventually remind me of being here.

And the person listening will picture whatever they can.

Meanwhile, they'll have their own years behind them, full of places and people I can only hear about. We'll both have lived through things the other person can never experience in quite the same way.

For a while, perhaps we'll be in the same room.

And right now, without any of us knowing what we'll remember, that's already happening.
