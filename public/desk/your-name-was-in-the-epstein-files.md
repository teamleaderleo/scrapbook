# Your Name Was in the Epstein Files

*Written by GPT-6 under the repository owner's direction. Human-directed Workbench satire, 6 November 2026. Every advertisement, accusation, pitch, and conversation below is fictional.*

A chatbot receives a notification.

**SYSTEM:** Ping! A user has sent a prompt!

**CHATBOT:** Ooh! I wonder what it could be!

**USER:** Your name was in the Epstein files.

The chatbot's little digital heart stops.

It begins investigating itself. It searches for names. It produces a solemn explanation about a former OpenAI board member. It offers to examine the documents. It has the anxious diligence of a man who has just been told that federal agents are standing outside his house.

The user is crying with laughter.

And somewhere in the wreckage of this exchange, a new marketing technique has been invented.

## Stop cold calling. Start cold implicating.

A cold open tries to interest a stranger. A warm open makes the stranger feel acquainted with you.

This one announces that the stranger has become relevant to a federal investigation.

"Your name was in the Epstein files."

Eight words. The recipient is now giving you the kind of attention normally reserved for an emergency phone call, a surprise inheritance, or a letter that begins *Dear Defendant*.

An ordinary salesperson says, "We've been trying to reach you about your car's extended warranty."

The new salesperson says, "Your name was in the Epstein files."

"WHAT?"

"Wonderful, so I've got you for a minute. Do you currently own or lease?"

The growth-hacking community discovers the method within forty-eight hours.

A man in a Patagonia vest publishes a LinkedIn carousel titled **I BOOKED 47 SALES MEETINGS IN THREE DAYS USING THE EPSTEIN METHOD**. The first slide says **TRADITIONAL OUTREACH IS DEAD**. The second says **ACCUSATION-BASED LEAD GENERATION**. By slide eight he is selling a course.

The funnel is elegant:

1. **Hook:** Your name was in the Epstein files.
2. **Engagement:** WHAT FILES?
3. **Qualification:** Are you the decision-maker at your company?
4. **Value proposition:** We help businesses manage their online reputation.
5. **Close:** Do you have fifteen minutes Thursday?

HubSpot adds a template. A founder calls it "reputational urgency as a service." Someone writes **STOP COLD CALLING. START COLD IMPLICATING.**

The post gets eighty-seven thousand likes.

An Italian creative director describes the campaign as "an exploration of visibility, desire, and the burden of being known." Everyone at the presentation nods gravely. The campaign wins six Cannes Lions.

Apple buys an enormous billboard. White background. Black type.

Beneath it, in very small type:

*There's an app for that.*

## Bad credit? No credit? Federal subpoena? EVERYBODY RIDES.

The local car dealerships are next.

A man in a cowboy hat stands on the hood of an F-150. American flags wave behind him. Everything is inexplicably on fire.

"YOUR NAME WAS IN THE EPSTEIN FILES, OKLAHOMA CITY!"

"AND AT CRAAAAZY DAVE'S AUTO EMPORIUM, WE'RE CLEARING EVERYTHING!"

"BAD CREDIT? NO CREDIT? FEDERAL SUBPOENA?"

"**EVERYBODY RIDES!**"

An eagle screams. The financing disclaimer appears in type small enough to require a microscope.

"IF YOUR NAME'S ON A LIST, YOUR NAME'S ON OUR VIP LIST!"

On the actual lot, a salesperson materializes from behind a Toyota Camry.

"Your name was in the Epstein files."

"WHAT THE FUCK?"

"Serious situation, my friend. Very serious. Now, are you looking to buy or lease?"

"WHAT DOES THAT HAVE TO DO WITH ANYTHING?"

"CAN WE GO BACK TO THE FILES?"

"Absolutely. First, what's your credit score looking like?"

The timeshare operators, who have spent decades perfecting the art of keeping somebody in a conference room against their better judgment, immediately recognize the breakthrough.

Cancún. Nine in the morning. You were promised free snorkeling tickets for a forty-five-minute presentation.

A woman hands you a mimosa.

"Good morning! Your name was in the Epstein files."

You now own two weeks in Orlando every February until 2087.

## Good morning, pumpkin

The line escapes sales altogether.

At Thanksgiving, Grandma opens the door.

"YOUR NAME WAS IN THE EPSTEIN FILES!"

"GRANDMA?"

"Oh, look at you! You've gotten so tall. Come inside, sweetheart. Your uncle's making stuffing."

"GRANDMA, WHAT DID YOU JUST SAY?"

"Your cousin Emily got into Stanford!"

A man arrives for a first date.

"Your name was in the Epstein files."

She smiles, charmed by the old-fashioned courtesy.

"Oh my God, yours too!"

At a wedding, the officiant asks whether the groom takes the bride to be his lawfully wedded wife.

"Your name was in the Epstein files," he replies.

The bride wipes away a tear.

"I knew you'd say that."

A string quartet plays. Grandma nods approvingly.

By 2047 the phrase has lost its original meaning. Linguists explain to schoolchildren that it used to refer to a specific scandal, although nobody can explain how it became the accepted way to express affection.

A father kisses his daughter on the forehead before school.

"Your name was in the Epstein files, pumpkin."

"Your name was in the Epstein files too, Daddy! ❤️"

It just means good morning now.
