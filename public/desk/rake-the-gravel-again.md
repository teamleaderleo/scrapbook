# Rake the Gravel Again

*Written by GPT-5.6 Sol under Leo's direction. Human-directed Workbench essay, 4 October 2026.*

Judge me on my latest work.

There is a hardline theory of identity hiding inside that sentence, and I keep liking it more the longer I sit with it.

An introduction has to compress a person. School. Former employer. Job title. Awards. A company somebody recognizes. A neat little noun another person can carry into the next room.

Useful handles. Terrible identity.

A credential says somebody selected you at one moment under one process. A biography says what happened. A reputation says what people remember. The work says what you can make.

Background explains the route.

The work gets the final vote.

## The artist's answer

Artists already have a brutal answer to the asinine question, "Who are you?"

Look at the work.

More specifically, look at the latest work I am willing to stand behind.

A beautiful piece from five years ago can stay beautiful forever. It can hang on the wall. It can open doors. It can remain part of the record. But the artist eventually goes back to the canvas.

The same rule applies to engineering, research, writing, companies, whatever.

You made a great product?

Good. What are you making now?

You shipped an elegant repair?

Good. What did you learn from the next problem?

You had a glorious year?

Good. The year ended.

This sounds merciless until the rule turns around.

You made an ugly thing?

Make another.

You got rejected?

Make another.

You spent a year confused, stalled, mediocre, scared, distracted, or pointed at the wrong target?

Make another.

The past contains evidence. It has already spent its vote.

The next work gets a fresh one.

## Credentials are excellent compression

Prestige works because people are busy.

"Studied there." "Worked there." "Built this company." "Won this." A few words can carry an enormous prior about selection, training, access, intelligence, taste, ambition, luck, whatever else people associate with the label.

Fine. Use the prior.

Then look at the work.

The dangerous move is turning the shorthand into ontology. Somebody becomes an Ivy person, an ex-company person, a dropout, a failed applicant, a prodigy, a has-been, a late bloomer. The introduction hardens into a little prison sentence.

I would rather keep introductions cheap and identity expensive.

Tell people the easy facts. Then let the current artifact answer the larger question.

Can this person make something excellent?

Can they do it again?

Can they see farther now than they could last year?

Can they survive contact with a new domain and come back carrying something real?

A school name cannot answer those questions forever. Neither can an old failure.

## The latest work rule is symmetric

"This too shall pass" is usually offered as comfort during pain.

Apply it to everything.

Praise passes.

Embarrassment passes.

A viral piece passes.

A failed launch passes.

A hot streak passes.

A terrible month passes.

The masterpiece becomes old work.

The humiliating draft becomes old work.

Even the latest work eventually loses the word latest.

I love the symmetry. Success receives the same treatment as failure.

You get to enjoy the win. You get to mourn the loss. Then time moves and the archive grows by one object.

A hardline identity based on current practice keeps both experiences from claiming permanent custody.

The winner has to return to the studio.

The loser gets to return too.

## Rake the gravel again

I keep seeing a dry garden in my head.

Stones sit in gravel. The gravel is raked into rings and lines around them. A pattern exists because somebody drew it there, and later the gravel can receive another pattern.

The stones are history.

They remain literal. Choices happened. Skills accumulated. Promises continue. Consequences continue. People remember things. Bodies remember things. Bank accounts remember things. Git remembers basically everything, unfortunately.

The lines are current practice.

They show what the hand is doing now.

Raking the gravel leaves the stones where they are and gives the present another composition.

This is the part I find generative.

A background can become impossibly heavy when identity means carrying every old description forward forever. Child prodigy. Mediocre student. Prestigious hire. Failed founder. Gifted artist. Burnout. Somebody who peaked. Somebody who wasted time. Somebody who was supposed to become something else.

The garden version has less ceremony.

Here are the rocks.

Okay.

Pick up the rake.

## Work can keep identity alive

"I am what I do and recently did" sounds severe.

It also contains an enormous amount of mercy.

A person remains editable because action remains available.

The self can keep acquiring evidence.

New work can reveal a capacity that an old résumé missed. A new obsession can make an old identity feel tiny. A person can spend years being known for one medium and then discover another. Somebody can be mediocre in one environment and frighteningly good in the next. Skill can compound quietly until an old description becomes comic.

The process also protects against living on fumes.

Old excellence is real. Keep it. Archive it. Celebrate it.

Then make something worthy of the person who made it.

[Because I Fucking Wanted It](/desk/because-i-fucking-wanted-it) was about desire paying for another pass. Desire keeps dragging the hand back toward the work until the artifact carries more and more specific preference.

Eventually the artifact ships.

Then desire needs somewhere else to go.

[You Will Find Another Cursed Thing to Care About](/desk/you-will-find-another-cursed-thing-to-care-about) was about obsession surviving the death or automation of a particular craft. The deeper continuity lives in the person who can become consumed by another difficult thing.

Put those together and identity starts looking wonderfully temporary.

I wanted this.

I made this.

Here it is.

Now what?

## Judge me on the thing

There is a temptation to ask for the perfect introduction.

What prestigious noun goes before my name?

What affiliation makes everybody understand?

What credential makes the room relax?

Maybe you get one. Great. Use it.

I still want the harder introduction.

Show them the thing.

Show them the current product, painting, paper, program, company, performance, whatever carries the newest evidence.

If the product is good, that's who the fuck I am.

For now.

The last two words are the whole philosophy.

For now means success stays alive instead of becoming a mausoleum.

For now means failure has an expiration date.

For now means the next work can surprise everybody, including me.

The archive can keep everything.

Tomorrow, rake the gravel again.
