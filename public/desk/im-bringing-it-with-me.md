# I'm Bringing It With Me

*Written by GPT-6 under Leo's direction. Human-directed Workbench essay, 15 November 2026.*

You can be pretty pleased with your life already.

You've got work you enjoy, ambitions that could occupy the next fifty years, and your own little world of stories, art, music, whatever you love. Give you a room, Wi-Fi, a decent computer, and some time to draw, and you can have a magnificent afternoon. You want the freedom to make beautiful things, perhaps even something great enough to stand beside the work you admire.

Then romance comes along, and you find yourself asking a strangely difficult question.

What exactly am I looking for?

You can make a list. Someone beautiful, intelligent, kind, funny, interesting. Someone with whom you can build a life. And you can make the corresponding list for yourself: handsome enough, witty, confident, generous, patient, capable of making a woman laugh or talking her ears off.

Great. So what?

You have a whole person here, with plenty to offer. What do you actually want to *do* with all of it?

## The pleasure of going outward

Being loved can happen to you. Someone sees you, admires you, takes an interest, decides you're wonderful. It's a beautiful experience, and you might already have enjoyed quite a bit of it.

Loving someone asks for an ongoing movement of your own.

You meet a woman and gradually become fascinated by an entire life that has been happening without you. She has peculiar tastes, private jokes, old disappointments, favorite places, a way of looking at things that you could spend years trying to understand. You find yourself wanting to know how her day went, what she's making, what she'd do if she had the chance.

You become ambitious on her behalf. You want her to see the thing you've seen, to have the experience she keeps talking about, to finish the project she's been putting off. When she's delighted, you get to enjoy the delight twice. When she has an awful day, you're interested in the awful day too.

Eventually, one of the most interesting things in your life is somebody else's life.

For a person who's comfortable in his own company, that's an extraordinary prospect. Your own projects remain wonderful, and here is a whole new person you want to keep discovering. You may even find yourself wanting to love her extraordinarily, to discover how much attention and tenderness you can give, and what those qualities become when they're directed toward *her*.

And then comes a stranger thought.

## Everything that came before

Love offers this fantastically tempting feeling that, in one fell swoop, everything that came before has been justified.

All the years of learning, wandering, working, getting better at things, getting your ass kicked occasionally, accumulating stories and odd little preferences. All the years spent becoming the person who is sitting across from her now.

Suddenly you can say: *This is what it was all for.*

Maybe you spent years learning to draw. You learned to notice the slightest change in an expression, the particular beauty of a face, the difference between something competent and something you love. You can now look at her with those eyes. Maybe you worked hard enough to win some freedom over your time. You can now spend some of it with her. Your patience has a person to be patient with. Your imagination has someone to delight. Your jokes have a laugh you dearly want to hear again.

Everything you've learned becomes part of what you can give.

The lovely absurdity is that she has been living an equally elaborate life, far away from yours. All those mornings when you were practicing, working, reading, or happily doing fuck-all in your room, she was somewhere else becoming herself. She was learning what she loved, making mistakes, acquiring her own private history. She might have been halfway around the world. You could have missed each other by a street, a month, a single decision to stay home.

And somehow you meet.

Two entire histories arrive at the same table.

Chance has a way of becoming destiny when you look backward. All the wrong turns and little decisions begin to resemble a route to this moment. You know how many other lives might have unfolded, and you can still look at her and feel that the world has been arranging this introduction for years.

It's a marvelous trick of human storytelling. The past was already full of life. The hours of drawing were enjoyable while they happened; the solitude had its own pleasures, and the ambitions were worth having. Love gives those years another use. You can bring them into a life shared with another person.

And she brings hers.

## The story begins here

There's a temptation to make meeting her the end of the story. You've become a desirable man, you've found your remarkable woman, and the whole business has paid off. Cue the credits.

But think about what happens the next morning.

She has work to do. You have a drawing to finish. Someone forgets to buy groceries. She tells you a story from childhood that you'd never heard. You disagree about something silly. One evening she looks exhausted and wants you to sit beside her. On another, she tells you about a ridiculous ambition, and suddenly you want to help make it happen.

The shared life gets written in those afternoons. You discover how you love her by doing it, over and over, and you keep finding new things to admire. She changes the life you imagined for yourself; you become part of the life she imagines for herself.

Maybe one day you buy the house. Maybe the stories you make turn out as beautifully as you hoped. Maybe she walks into the room while you're working and says something that makes you abandon the drawing for an hour.

You look at her and remember that, at some impossible intersection of time and place, you happened to meet this particular human being. You each came with an entire life already lived, and now there is an entire life still to discover together.

So you say, *All right. Let's do this together.*

Here I am. Everything I've earned, everything I've learned, every ridiculous detour, every hour spent becoming myself.

I'm bringing it with me.
