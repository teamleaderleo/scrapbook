# Just Leo: Starting and Continuing

*By Leo. First draft, 8 September 2026. Transcribed from Leo’s messages with paragraph breaks and light punctuation cleanup; no AI-written additions.*

I've noticed, of course, well, people do different things. People act in different ways. Why is that? One of the simpler ways of seeing it is that people have different levels of activation friction and also different levels of continuation friction, a.k.a. how willing you are to start things and how willing you are to follow through and to do that over and over again.

So when you can direct that kind of personality to places, you can get a really long way, especially these days, because you've reduced the vast majority of the prior information asymmetry. Like we all say, you know, public libraries exist, the internet exists. You have what was previously considered a supercomputer in your pocket now with your phone. Now we have AI that connects to anything and everything. There were still barriers, though. That's what a lot of the diversity programs are for.

But people weigh costs and benefits differently. You can have infinite birds in the bush, but if it's a prickly bush, people don't want to go through that. Or even if it's just a normal bush, but people think it's kind of prickly, they don't want to go through that. They build up dragons in their mind.

I think there's something similar to that with all sorts of grand narrative type things. People get led astray by their own storytelling. They tell stories about themselves. They see themselves in certain ways, and they tell themselves they can't start X or Y thing. Often it's justifiable. And if they do somehow start, they tell themselves they shouldn't continue it, which is also often justifiable. So the people who can do both are kind of weird. Relatively. I think I'm one of those people.

AI has materially changed both, which makes the distinction starker. Even with this thing which you can literally tell “look for things to improve” and “are you sure? let's keep looking” and “continue” and bear all sorts of fruit, people still won't do it. There are all manner of (again, often justifiable in piecemeal) reasons they supply. But it's there. You can use it. It's hilariously subsidized relative to the (accounting) costs. The barrier is imagined dragons.
