# Until I Can't See You Anymore

*Written by GPT-6 under Leo's direction. Human-directed Workbench essay, 2 November 2026.*

You're walking on different paths, with plenty of space between you. Each of you has somewhere else to go.

You look up.

They're looking at you.

You keep walking. They keep walking.

Their head turns.

Yours does too.

Oh, fuck.

You've passed each other now. You're both looking back over your shoulders, trying to steal another second from the distance opening between you.

*Oh. You're exactly my type.*

*Oh, you're looking back.*

*Oh no. I have to go.*

*Let me look at you for as long as I can.*

You could stop. Turn around. Risk the ridiculous hello. But your feet keep going, even as your eyes ask you to do something else.

Another step. You're both still looking. It's become a ridiculous way to walk.

And then the angle gives out.

Your head turns forward. The stranger is behind you. The street goes on.

In another life, one of you turns around.

"Hey. Sorry. This is weird."

They laugh.

Later, you hear that laugh from another room and smile before you even know what's funny.

You know the sound of their footsteps in the hallway.

One morning they walk into the kitchen asking where their keys are. You look up and see the face you once twisted your neck to keep in view, standing there with sleep in their eyes and a hand on their hip. You saw the keys by the door.

You've looked at this face on mornings you can barely remember. You get to look again tomorrow.

Years later, somebody asks how you met.

"We were walking past each other. I almost kept going."

In the life you lived, both of you kept walking.

The imaginary life gets to stay luminous. Your mind takes a face and a lingering look and builds twenty years. Then it asks you to miss them.

At the next block, you try to remember the stranger's face. You remember their expression, the turn of their head, how long they kept looking. The rest is already slipping. By evening you might have only the feeling of being looked at, and of wanting to look a little longer.

And then another one.

A different day. Another pair of eyes. Again, somebody turns their head.

How many lifetimes live and die in those intervals?

Maybe tomorrow, one of you will turn all the way around.

Today, for the little time you have, you look at each other.

Another step.

One more second.

*Until I can't see you anymore.*
