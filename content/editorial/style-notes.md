# Rolling style notes

_Last updated: 2026-07-26_

These notes collect recurring habits noticed during editorial review. Check new agent drafts against them before filing.

## Current redlines

### Formulaic declarations

Watch for sentences that announce a conclusion with unnecessary ceremony:

- `The mistake is ...`
- `The answer is ...`
- `The surprise comes from ...`
- `The real cost is ...`
- `This is where ...`

Prefer the actual observation. Name the actor, action, or consequence directly.

### Repeated “the X of Y” phrasing

Noun-heavy constructions can create a false sense of weight, especially when several appear close together. Rewrite toward verbs and concrete subjects. Keep an `X of Y` phrase when it is the clearest wording rather than a reflex.

### Unearned grandeur

Do not turn routine workflow advice into a manifesto. A large cadence, solemn metaphor, or dramatic final line needs either a genuinely large subject or a joke strong enough to justify it.

### Melodrama needs a punchline

Funny melodrama is welcome. Straight-faced melodrama that merely inflates the subject should be cut. The joke should be legible without asking the reader to admire the sentence.

### Metaphors have a budget

Choose one useful metaphor and let it do limited work. Do not stack launch ceremony, theatre, copy desk, institutional memory, and publication machinery into the same short essay unless each adds information.

### Self-redline before filing

Before opening a PR:

1. Search for `The mistake`, `The answer`, `This is where`, and similar declarations.
2. Mark every abstract noun phrase that could become a verb.
3. Cut any conclusion that restates the previous paragraph at a higher emotional volume.
4. Check whether the funniest line is actually funny.
5. Keep the writer's identity and draft status visible.
